DIGITAL PRINTING

Digital printing is a modern method of production that makes prints from electronic files. It involves your artwork being created on a computer and then printed directly onto the material of your choice. Digital printing is an alternative to traditional methods such as lithography, flexography, gravure, letter press, and others – it eliminates many of the mechanical steps required for conventional printing, such as making films and color proofs, manually stripping the pieces together, and making plates.
Get Started

HOW IT WORKS

With the method of digital printing, an image is sent directly to the printer using digital files in PDF, TIFF, PSD or other formats. Digital printing is best used for items that require high amounts of detail and smaller quantity orders. Unlike conventional printing, there are no pre-press stages between the digital document files and the final product; there is also no need for messy formatting equipment like film plates or photo chemicals.

Digital prints should come out perfectly when the following stages of production are done accurately.

BENEFITS OF DIGITAL PRINTING:

High Quality: This offers impressive quality and consistency over the other options. The colors show up perfectly on the prints and there are no issues with harsh lines. With digital printing, the quality of the last card, brochure or flyer in a batch is same as that of the first.

Timeliness: There are fewer steps in the printing process, and as a result, the final product can be delivered quicker.

Cost Effective: Because printing plates are not required, there is less investment involved to set up a single job.

Short Runs: Digital printing is the ideal method of producing short to medium print runs compared to traditional methods.

Customization: Digital printing provides the most affordable solution to customize marketing materials, direct mail pieces and letters, business cards, and more.

OFFSET PRINTING

Offset printing is a printing technique that involves the transferring of an image from a metal plate to a rubber blanket or rollers before being printed on the receiving media, usually paper. In this method, the paper does not come in direct contact with the metal plates. Offset printing is also known as offset lithography.
Get Started

The Benefits of Offset Printing

High quality and true color: Since Offset uses ink instead of toner (digital printing), the result of the print is a lot richer and clearer

Low cost: Medium to large quantity printing is best used with offset printing. The more quantities that are produced, the cheaper the per unit cost becomes.

Fast and efficient: Web-fed can produce over 80,000 copies of printed materials while producing them in high quality

Flexible and dynamic: Offset printing works on a wide range of materials including paper, cardboard, wood, leather, and plastic while providing solutions to special finishes and other design requests.

For many customers, this is a great way to get full-color prints on just a few shirts. Also referred to as Direct-To Garment or Print On Demand, the process works much like a traditional inkjet computer printer, but for apparel!

  • Yields high-quality, durable images.
  • Print on any color garment recommended for dark prints on light color garments for best result.
  • Price: Fixed cost per shirt, regardless of quantity ordered. Often cheaper than screen printing for multiple-color images on small runs.
  • Used for: Photos, Graphic Effects, and highly detailed images on small runs of shirts.

What's the difference between screen printing and digital printing?

Screen printing involves creating a stencil, known as a screen, and then using that screen to apply ink on the printing surface (in our case, apparel or promotional products). Each individual color is applied using a different screen, one at a time, combined to achieve the final look. The more colors a design has, the more screens that are needed, and the more setup time required. The ink in screen printing is applied thicker than digital printing, which results in brighter colors – even on darker shirts. Screen printing is the best option for designs that require a high level of vibrancy, specialty products, or for quantities greater than 6 pieces. While Print Media Online has no minimums for screen printing, price is usually a prohibitive factor under 6 pieces. Over 6 pieces, the per piece cost tends to be less than Digital printing (but not always).

Digital printing or Direct to Garment (DTG) is a much newer process that involves your artwork being processed by a computer and then printed directly onto the surface of your product. DTG is not a transfer – the ink directly adheres to the fabric of your custom shirt. The fact that the design is processed and printed digitally allows for a quantity of one since there are no screens or physical setup. This method is best used for items that require large amounts of detail and/or print colors, customers that require water-based ink, and for orders of a smaller quantity (5 or fewer pieces). The fact that the digital printer does not use screens allows for a photographic print, with much more detail than traditional screen printing. As the ink is applied thinner (to achieve such detail), digital printing is best used on lighter-colored shirts to allow the design to shine through.

Personalized Pens for Your Business

When you give your promotional pens to employees, clients or potential customers, you’re providing them with a much-needed tool that’s sure to come in handy before too long. They’ll remember your generosity every time they pull out their new pen, keeping your brand right in the forefront of their mind.

One of the great things about promotional pens is that you can customize them in any way you want. Opt for ink colors that fit your business aesthetic or go with the traditional black. You can also choose from affordable pens made from plastic, aluminum, brass, bamboo or a combination of materials. Our variety includes hundreds of styles and many colors and brands. 

Whatever you choose, company pens are a perfect choice for a last-minute trade show or a gift for valued employees.

Types of Custom Office Pens

All of our pens are great for your customers and employees to make lists for the grocery store, journal at the end of the day or keep track of important information. But many of them can do so much more, from cleaning screens to doubling as a phone stand. Our top pen categories include:

  • Classic pens: You’ve probably seen the popular javelin pen in banks or handed out at fairs. Now’s your chance to customize this classic model with your business logo and colors for a gift everyone will love receiving.
  • Multifunctional pens: These pens might aid recipients in many ways, just like your company. Choose from pens with multiple ink colors, stylus pens, and pens with built-in bottle openers.
  • Gel pens: Give your customers that smooth feel of gel ink across their checks, notes and lists with our stylized gel pens, many of which include textured grips.
  • Novelty pens: Looking to appeal to a younger crowd? Trying to add a playful tone to your brand? Novelty pens are the way to go! Our novelty pens might light up, have silly faces or include stress balls. They’re sure to stand out in the crowd.
  • Highlighters and markers: Speaking of standing out, make your company’s personalized markers and highlighters a bright spot in someone’s day. 
  • Gift sets: Give your brand’s supporters a token of your appreciation that will keep you on their minds for years to come. Notebook combos, sticky flag sets and custom ballpoint pens are all sure to impress.

How Many Custom Business Pens Should I Buy?

When you find the perfect promotional pens for your campaign, your next step will be deciding how many you’ll need. This answer varies based on how you’ll be distributing them. If you’re keeping them at the office and handing them out to visitors, try to estimate the foot traffic your business receives over time and go from there.

If you’re planning on taking your personalized pens to trade shows, conventions and other events, it’s helpful to have a rough estimate of expected attendance. At large events where guests may not be able to get to every table in the venue, having enough pens for about 25% of the crowd should be sufficient. At smaller events where you’ll have the chance to talk to a larger percentage of people, aim to have enough pens for around 75% of them.
